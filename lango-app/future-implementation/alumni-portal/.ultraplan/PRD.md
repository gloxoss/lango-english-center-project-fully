# Alumni Portal — Product Requirements

> Generated by UltraPlan | 2026-08-06
> This document describes what we're building in plain English.
> For the technical implementation plan, see PLAN.md.

## What We're Building
A real, self-service portal for former students. When a school marks a student as graduated, they get their own separate login to a new part of the system where they can see their real records, verify/request documents, join alumni events, optionally appear in a searchable alumni directory, and offer to mentor current students or other alumni — all built on real, permanent data, not a mock-up. The fake alumni page that already exists in the system (hardcoded names, fake events, a donations card) is fully replaced by this.

## The Problem
Right now, when a student leaves the school, their access is either left dangling (a live student login with no reason to exist) or simply forgotten. The school has no real way to stay in touch with former students, verify a graduate's documents for an employer, or reconnect them for events or mentoring — and the one "Alumni Portal" screen that exists today shows made-up names and fake statistics, not anything real. This plan replaces that with a real graduate lifecycle: access changes cleanly at graduation, and everything after that point is built on real, permanent, opt-in data.

## Who It's For
**Alumni** (former students) are the primary user of the new self-service portal — they log in with their own separate account to see their records, manage their own privacy settings, and optionally connect with the school community.
**School administrators** manage the transition from student to alumni, review document correction/reissue requests, and see the real alumni directory/events from the admin side — no new staff role, the same admins who already manage students handle this.
**Employers or other third parties** get a narrow, anonymous slice of this: a public page where they can check whether a document is genuine, without needing any account or seeing anything else.

## What It Does

### Must Have
- **Graduation transition**: an admin marks a specific student as alumni through a real, deliberate confirmation step — their student login stops working, and a real alumni invitation (reusing the same temp-password/invite-link system already used for admission) gets them into their new account.
- **Alumni self-service login**: a real, separate account and portal — not a repurposed student login.
- **Real records access**: alumni see their own real academic record and can download real, verified copies of their documents.
- **Document verification**: every issued document gets a real, unique verification code. Anyone — an employer, for example — can check that code on a public page (no login needed) and get a simple "genuine" or "not found" answer, without ever seeing the actual file. If a document is corrected and reissued, the old code stops working.
- **Correction & reissue requests**: alumni can ask for a mistake to be fixed or a lost document to be reissued — a real staff member reviews and approves it, alumni can never edit their own official record directly.
- **Alumni events**: the school posts real events, alumni can RSVP.
- **Opt-in directory**: alumni choose, field by field, what other alumni can see about them (name, cohort, current employer, contact info, etc.) — nothing is shown unless they've explicitly turned it on, and they can turn it back off at any time.
- **Mentoring listing**: alumni who want to can list themselves as open to mentoring or volunteering; anyone browsing can see the list and reach out directly — no automated matching.
- **Data requests**: alumni can ask to see, correct, or delete their own optional community data (directory listing, preferences) — their real academic record stays protected by law and isn't deleted, and they're told why.
- **Minors safeguarding**: alumni who are still under 18 are automatically excluded from the directory and mentoring listing until they turn 18, even though they've already graduated.

### Should Have
- Real announcements alumni see when they log in, also sent as a real text-message notification (using the same honest, logged text-message system already used elsewhere in this app).
- Handling for the rare case of a former alumnus being re-admitted as a new student — their alumni access pauses automatically until they graduate again.

### Nice to Have (explicitly deferred, not built in this plan)
- Donations and fundraising — the source plan for this feature explicitly defers this until legal, payment, and accounting requirements are separately worked out.
- An automated mentor-matching system (someone requesting a specific mentor and the mentor accepting/declining) — this plan builds the simpler real listing instead.
- A general-purpose school events system — this plan builds alumni's own real events, not a shared system with the rest of the school (that's a separate, not-yet-started plan).

## How It Should Feel
The admin-side screens (managing the transition, reviewing requests) look and feel exactly like every other admin screen in this system already does — same colors, same table layouts, same side-panel details. The new alumni-facing portal is for a different audience seeing the system for the first time, but should still feel like it belongs to the same product, not a different app bolted on. The public document-verification page is deliberately minimal — no login, no navigation, just a clear yes/no answer.

## What It Connects To
Nothing outside the system itself. Reuses the exact same login-credential system already used for new-student accounts, the same text-message notification system, and the same file-storage system already handling other school documents. No new outside services.

## What It Does NOT Do
- Does not accept or process donations/fundraising of any kind.
- Does not let alumni directly edit their own official academic record — every correction goes through a real staff review.
- Does not build an automated mentor-matching or request/accept workflow — just a real opt-in list.
- Does not show anyone under 18 in the directory or mentoring list, no matter what they've opted into.
- Does not delete legally-required academic records, even if an alumnus asks — only their optional community data.
- Does not touch or depend on the separate, not-yet-built general school events system.

## How We'll Know It Works
- A student marked as alumni can no longer log into their old student account, and can log into a real new alumni account instead.
- A document's real verification code correctly confirms as genuine on the public page — and stops working the moment that document is reissued.
- An alumnus's directory visibility exactly matches what they've individually turned on, nothing more.
- No alumnus under 18 ever appears in the directory or mentoring list, verified with a real test case.
- A deletion request removes exactly the optional data it should and never touches the protected academic record.
- Nothing from this plan breaks any existing student, admission, or promotion feature already working in this system.

## Business Model
Not applicable — this is a feature of the existing school system, not a separately monetized product. Donations (the one feature here with a real monetization angle) are explicitly out of scope for this plan.

## Risks & Concerns
1. **Changing a person's role from student to alumni has never been done anywhere in this system before.** Every other role is set once, when an account is first created, and never changed. This is genuinely new, careful logic — worth building slowly and testing thoroughly rather than rushing, since a mistake here could either leave a graduated student's old access open or lock a real alumnus out of their new one.
2. **Forgetting even one of three separate places where a new user role has to be registered would silently break every alumnus's ability to log in at all**, even though everything else about the feature would look correctly built. This is a real, easy-to-miss failure mode being planned around explicitly, not discovered after the fact.
3. **The public document-verification page has no login by design, which means it needs its own real protection against abuse** (someone trying every possible code, for example) — reusing the same real rate-limiting already protecting this system's other public page, not inventing new protection from scratch.
